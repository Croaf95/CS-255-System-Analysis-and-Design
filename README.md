##CS 255 System Analysis and Design
##DriverPass Project

This repository contains artifacts from the DriverPass system analysis and design project completed for CS 255 at Southern New Hampshire University. The project focuses on analyzing business requirements and designing a software solution that addresses a client's business needs through structured systems analysis and design methodologies.

##Project Reflection

##Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

The DriverPass project involved analyzing the needs of DriverPass, a driver education company seeking to improve student success rates on DMV examinations. The client wanted a cloud-based web application that would allow customers to access training materials, complete practice exams, purchase lesson packages, schedule driving lessons, and track their progress. The system also needed to support internal users, including instructors, secretaries, administrators, and IT personnel through role-based access and management features. The overall goal was to create a centralized platform that would improve driver education and increase DMV test pass rates.

##What did you do particularly well?

One of my strongest contributions was translating the client's business requirements into a detailed system design. I effectively identified both functional and nonfunctional requirements, including security, performance, accessibility, and scalability needs. I also developed UML diagrams that clearly illustrated system interactions, workflows, class relationships, and sequence processes. These models helped create a comprehensive design that aligned technical solutions with business objectives. Additionally, I focused on designing a cloud-based architecture that supported reliability, future growth, and ease of maintenance.

##If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I were to revise one area of this project, I would further expand the technical design portion of the system documentation. While the diagrams and technical requirements successfully met the project objectives, I would add more detailed database specifications, interface mockups, and data flow diagrams. These additions would provide developers with a clearer implementation roadmap and create a more complete picture of how information moves throughout the system. Expanding these areas would improve the overall quality and professionalism of the design documentation.

##How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?

I interpreted user needs by carefully analyzing the DriverPass interview and identifying the challenges the organization was trying to solve. The resulting system design incorporated features such as online practice testing, lesson scheduling, customer account management, progress tracking, instructor scheduling, reporting capabilities, and secure access controls. Each design decision was tied directly to a user or business requirement. Considering user needs is essential because a system's success depends on how effectively it solves real problems for its users. Even a technically advanced system can fail if it does not provide a practical and intuitive experience for those who use it daily.

##How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

My approach to software design begins with understanding the business problem and gathering detailed stakeholder requirements. I then identify functional and nonfunctional requirements and use modeling techniques such as UML use case, activity, sequence, and class diagrams to visualize system behavior and structure. This project reinforced the value of creating clear documentation before implementation begins. In future projects, I will continue using requirements analysis, object-oriented design principles, modeling tools, user-centered design practices, and iterative review processes to create systems that are both technically sound and aligned with stakeholder expectations.
